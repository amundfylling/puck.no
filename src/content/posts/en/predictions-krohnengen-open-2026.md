## 10. Hans Christian Lønne

Monte Carlo simulations and thorough analysis are part of his toolkit, and it is hard not to respect a player who takes preparation so seriously. The problem is that table hockey sometimes refuses to follow the spreadsheet. A bounce-out, an own goal, or an Arne Hetlelid in the zone can quickly ruin even the most beautiful model. I predict HC will just sneak into the top 10, but I think there will be some discussion about whether the probability calculations really accounted for human madness.

## 9. Ane Skadsem

Ane is a player I think several opponents could struggle with if they go into their matches a little too relaxed. She has shown she can challenge experienced opponents, and at Norway Open she even made Joachim Thyzell come from behind in a round-of-16 series after losing the first game. Those are promising signs. The next step is to keep the pressure on throughout the series, even when her opponent starts finding answers. I think Ane can spring a surprise, but that the playoffs will stop her before medals really come into the picture.

## 8. Sinus Moberg

He took Vegard Klippen all the way to a seventh and deciding game at Norway Open, showing that he can match much more experienced opponents. He still lacks a little experience in the decisive moments, but he certainly does not lack enthusiasm. Sinus is not the type to celebrate an important goal with a modest nod, either. Here you get the full package! I think he will have a good tournament, but that experience will ultimately make the difference when the really big matches are decided.

## 7. Benjamin Nygård

He does not rely on a single feint to cause problems, and that is an important quality in a strong field. I think the challenge will be finding the right balance between patience and risk. When his attack is flowing, he can beat most players at this level. When his finishing is off, it will be important not to start forcing solutions. I predict a solid seventh place, but I suspect Benjamin would like to prove he deserves more than that.

## 3rd place: Arne Hetlelid

Arne is a player who can go from a friendly chat to full competition mode in an impressively short time. In Trondheim, he came back to win a quarterfinal series against Daniel Nygård after trailing 1–3 in games. That says a lot about both his fighting spirit and his ability to handle pressure. The fast Bergen boards suit him well, too, and once he gets into a rhythm, the goals tend to come at both ends. This is also where his weakness lies: Arne rarely plays boring hockey, but that is not always the same as playing without risk. I think he will once again be a real nuisance for the favorites, and that he will make it all the way to the semifinal before his run ends.

## 2. Daniel Nygård

Daniel is the field's analyst, tactician, and probably the man who has thought the most about which opponent is best suited to which board. He likes to understand the game in detail and find the small advantages others overlook. That can be an enormous strength in a long tournament. At the same time, there is always a danger that the analyses become a little too important when things start going wrong. It does not help that the simulation predicted a win if his opponent suddenly decides to play the best table hockey of their life. The quarterfinal loss to Hetlelid in Trondheim showed that even a good position can unravel. This time I think Daniel will make it all the way to the final, but will have to settle for silver.

## 1. Andreas Fjermestad

I predict Andreas will win Krohnengen Open 2026. He has a dangerous attack, and his left shovels can be almost unstoppable when his timing is right. He has also shown that he can raise his game in the playoffs, where small margins often decide the outcome. The challenge is that the same set of weapons does not always work equally well from one match to the next. When the left shovels are working, he looks like a world champion; when they are not, it can quickly turn into a frustrating afternoon. Still, I think he has enough consistency and variety to get through the difficult spells. This time he goes all the way!
